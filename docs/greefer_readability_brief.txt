# Infiniglyph — Greefer Animation and Combat Readability Pass

## Objective

Greefer, our enormous neon-wireframe griffon boss, is now implemented and functional. He dives from Central Tower, approaches and pursues the player, attacks, deals damage, and responds to victory or defeat.

However, **his attack animations are not visibly readable during gameplay**. The player takes substantial damage without being able to identify which attack Greefer performed or when the attack connected.

This is primarily an **animation and combat presentation issue**, not a request to rebuild his AI or movement systems.

Our combat design combines the mobility, aerial freedom, and fast exchanges of *Shinobi Striker* with the deliberate, punishing, timing-based boss encounters of *Dark Souls*.

## 1. Inspect the Existing Implementation

Before changing anything:

- Identify Greefer's existing attack types, state machine, damage events, hitboxes, and animation structure.
- Determine whether animations are missing, not playing, too subtle, or disconnected from combat events.
- Reuse the existing procedural wireframe model and combat logic wherever practical.
- Preserve his Central Tower entrance, flight, pursuit, rooftop navigation, and rematch functionality.

Do not rewrite working systems unnecessarily.

## 2. Create Distinct, Visible Attack Animations

Every existing damaging attack needs a recognizable animation.

Examples, where applicable:

- **Claw swipe:** Raise a foreleg, rotate the shoulder, sweep the claws through the target area, then recover.
- **Beak strike:** Retract the neck, thrust the head forward, then return to a neutral position.
- **Wing sweep:** Extend and rotate the wing through a wide arc with visible torso movement.
- **Dive attack:** Fold or reposition the wings, commit to the dive, strike or land, and visibly recover.
- **Charge or body attack:** Shift weight, accelerate, commit to the movement, and decelerate naturally.

These are examples, not instructions to invent unnecessary attacks. Animate the moves already implemented first.

Ensure the head, neck, wings, legs, and torso articulate independently where appropriate. Greefer must not look like a rigid sculpture sliding through attack states.

Preserve his cyan-and-magenta neon wireframe appearance.

## 3. Implement Readable Boss Attack Timing

Each attack should have three distinguishable phases:

**Windup:** A visible physical tell indicating that an attack is coming.

**Active:** The actual striking motion, during which the appropriate hitbox becomes active.

**Recovery:** A visible follow-through and period of commitment before Greefer can begin another major action.

The player must be able to learn attack timing through observation.

Different attacks should have different timings based on their speed, reach, and damage. Avoid making every attack use identical animation durations.

Do not simply slow down all of Greefer's combat. Preserve his aggression while making attacks readable.

## 4. Synchronize Damage With Animation

This is a critical requirement.

- Damage must correspond to the visible strike and its collision window.
- Avoid invisible damage, repeated damage from a single unintended hit, or hitboxes remaining active throughout an entire animation.
- Ensure hitboxes follow the correct attacking body parts.
- Preserve existing block, parry, dodge, and exhaustion interactions.
- Ensure that missed attacks do not damage the player.
- Test attack collision at different elevations and during aerial combat.

Where practical, drive hitbox activation through animation events or equivalent deterministic attack-phase logic.

## 5. Preserve Boss Attack Commitment

Unlike the player's highly flexible PvP-oriented combat, major boss attacks should involve meaningful commitment.

Greefer may track the player during an appropriate windup period, but once a major strike commits, he should not automatically redirect it to guarantee a hit.

Players must be able to:

- Read an attack.
- Dodge, parry, block, jump, or reposition.
- Cause the attack to miss through correct timing.
- Exploit suitable recovery opportunities.

Greefer should remain intelligent and dangerous, but not artificially unavoidable.

## 6. Improve Creature Movement and Reactions

Add or refine the necessary secondary animations:

- Grounded idle breathing and weight shifts.
- Walking and running leg movement.
- Wing flapping, gliding, folding, and takeoff transitions.
- Turning and repositioning without excessive rigid-body sliding.
- Landing impact and recovery.
- Appropriate hit reactions and stagger animations.

Minor hits should not necessarily interrupt an enormous boss. Reactions should scale appropriately with attack impact and existing combat mechanics.

Keep movement transitions smooth without breaking navigation.

## 7. Maintain Readability During Aerial Combat

Infiniglyph allows unrestricted Sky Jump movement, so players may fight Greefer from above, below, or beside him.

His animations must remain identifiable from multiple camera angles.

Use clear silhouettes and large physical motions rather than relying exclusively on tiny effects or ground-based warning indicators.

Greefer should remain capable of fighting airborne players, but those attacks must be visually understandable.

## 8. Testing and Acceptance Criteria

Test Greefer in actual gameplay, not only through automated damage checks.

Verify that:

1. Every existing damaging attack has a visible animation.
2. Damage occurs during the appropriate visible contact window.
3. Windup, active, and recovery phases are distinguishable.
4. Players can successfully evade attacks through timing.
5. Greefer can miss and recover without automatically correcting every committed attack.
6. Ground and aerial attacks both remain functional.
7. Movement, pursuit, Central Tower entrance, and encounter progression remain intact.
8. The model's limbs, head, and wings visibly articulate rather than moving as one rigid object.
9. No major regressions are introduced into existing combat systems.

If possible, add a temporary developer option to display Greefer's current attack name, phase, and active hitboxes for debugging. Keep this disabled in normal gameplay.

## Implementation Priority

First, fix one existing attack end-to-end, including animation, hitbox synchronization, and recovery. Verify it works, then apply the same approach to the remaining attacks.

Prioritize readable combat over adding more attacks, particle effects, or cosmetic complexity.

**Desired outcome:** When Greefer defeats the player, they should understand what hit them and have a reasonable opportunity to learn how to avoid it next time.

The goal is not to make Greefer easy. The goal is to make him difficult, visually expressive, and fair.

Please inspect the existing project, implement the changes, run available tests, and summarize what was changed and what still requires manual playtesting.
